# Session Design Patterns and Fixes

Catalog of common session design problems, each with symptom, diagnosis, and fix. Companion to `../SKILL.md`, which owns the core principles and the five-pass review process.

## Pattern: Lazy opening that primes defensiveness

**Symptom:** Opening chat question measures participants' output ("how many times did you do X?")

**Why it's broken:** Triggers defensiveness, performance, or anxiety in the first 30 seconds — before any safety has been established.

**Fix:** Switch to a reflective question that grounds in their experience and signals "your reality matters here." Examples:
- "What's been the hardest part of [thing] these past [period]?"
- "One word for how [experience] has felt"
- "What's surprised you about [thing]?"

## Pattern: "Co-creation" that's actually reactive

**Symptom:** You're asking participants to "shape" something but really they're voting on your framework

**Why it's broken:** Participants feel consulted, not creative. Real agency is generative.

**Fix:** Have them generate first, then reveal/compare. The compare moment can include "what's missing from our version" zones so they can challenge the framework, not just rate it.

## Pattern: Hard messages delivered before shared data

**Symptom:** "You should be doing X independently" delivered in the opening; then participants are asked to share what they've actually done

**Why it's broken:** The message reads as judgment of the past. Sharing becomes a confession.

**Fix:** Move the message to AFTER the shared-data moment. Same content, different timing. It now reads as a forward commitment grounded in shared reality.

## Pattern: Talking points all crammed in one section

**Symptom:** Section 2 has 4 talking points; section 5 just has Mariena's intro

**Why it's broken:** Some of the 4 talking points belong at different moments in the arc — and crammed in upfront, the harder ones poison the well.

**Fix:** Audit each talking point for where it primes well. Split across sections. Often the acknowledgment-style messages land early; the expectation-setting messages land after the generative moment.

## Pattern: Group debrief with implausible timing

**Symptom:** "6 pods surface one barrier each in 1 min"

**Why it's broken:** 10 seconds per pod = not actual surfacing. Either skip the debrief or budget realistic time.

**Fix:** If surfacing matters, budget 1 min per pod minimum. If you don't have that time, redesign the activity to capture data on whiteboard (asynchronous surfacing) without requiring verbal share.

## Pattern: Activity that surfaces the wrong data

**Symptom:** Pair stress-test stays polite; group brainstorm produces generalities; "any questions?" gets silence

**Why it's broken:** Format mismatched to need. Pair stress-tests need a structural reason to push back (Troika silent-client variation). Brainstorms need anchored prompts. Questions need invitation patterns.

**Fix:** Change the format, not the prompt. Pick the activity that does the work you actually need.
