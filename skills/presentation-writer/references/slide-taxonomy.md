# Slide Taxonomy

The full catalog of Mellonhead slide types, plus the arc slide sequences and in-person adaptations. Load this before drafting any slides. Use the taxonomy names when drafting ("Problem slide", "Your turn", "Best practices") so the structure is explicit before visual design.

---

## Slide Types

### 1. Title Slide
- Workshop series label (small, bold, teal): "The Chiefs AI Workshop #1:"
- Workshop name (very large, gray): "AI Foundations & Key Use Cases"
- Facilitator: "with **Mariena Quintanilla**"
- Date at bottom left
- {{client program}} logo bottom left
- Hero image right half
- **For in-person workshops:** add a prominent QR code on the right side pointing to the shared materials folder. Participants scan as they arrive. Keep the code visible during early segments in case anyone needs to re-scan.

### 2. Section Divider
- Section label (small, bold, blue): "Activity 1" / "Demo" / "Closing activity"
- Section name (very large, gray): "Email synthesis" / "Copilot agents"
- Tagline (small, light gray): "Stop searching, start asking" / "From experimentation to direction"
- Hero image right half
- **Every activity and major section gets one of these.**

### 3. Purpose / Framework Slide
- Title (large, gray)
- 2-3 columns, each with: icon + bold colored label + one-line description
- Example: "Our purpose" with three columns: A clearer mental model / Personal use / Informed strategy

### 4. Problem Slide
- Title: "The Problem: [Noun]"
- Two icon + label pairs showing contrast: pain state vs. AI-assisted state
- Example: "Tedious work recreating context" vs. "Quickly pull together information based on context"
- No body paragraphs. Just the contrast.

### 5. Approach / Process Slide
- Title: "How to [verb] [object]" or "The approach" or "Simple approach"
- Numbered steps (01, 02, 03, 04) with colored top-bar separators
- Each step: **Bold label** + 1-2 lines of description
- Optional: colored content blocks above the steps for key framing (e.g., "Chat vs. Outlook" distinction)

### 6. Tool Orientation Slide
- Title: "How to Access [Tool]"
- Screenshots with labels
- 2-3 options side by side with descriptions
- Most text-heavy slide type. Acceptable because screenshots carry visual weight.

### 7. Your Turn Slide
- Split layout: stock photo left (woman at laptop), instructions right
- Title: "Your turn"
- Bold setup lines + bulleted questions or numbered steps
- Timer icon + time in large text: "3 minutes" / "5 minutes"
- **Always uses the same stock photo for consistency across activities.**

### 7b. Now Try Again Slide (Two-Attempt only)
- Same split layout as Your Turn: stock photo left (same woman at laptop), instructions right
- Title: **"Now try again"** (never "Your turn": the distinct title signals this is a retry)
- Numbered steps reference what they learned: "Start a new conversation", "Write an updated prompt"
- Always includes "ask for help" fallback
- Timer icon + time in large text: shorter than original Your Turn (typically 3-5 min vs. 4-8 min)
- **Same stock photo as Your Turn** for visual consistency
- Only appears in two-attempt activities, after the Let's Reflect (Keep/Change/Add) slide

### 8. Let's Share Slide (Two-Attempt, after Try 1)
- Title: "Let's share"
- Three cards with colored left borders (maroon, blue, green):
  - **What?** What happened? What did you get? (e.g., "Did AI stick to facts or add its own interpretation?")
  - **So what?** Where did the output help? Where didn't it?
  - **Now what?** Where else could you use this? (connects to their broader work)
- Used during the compare/discuss segment after the first attempt in two-attempt activities.
- **Not a final reflection.** This surfaces insights that feed into the Show and second attempt.

### 8b. Let's Reflect Slide, Keep/Change/Add (Two-Attempt, after Show)
- Title: "Let's reflect"
- Subtitle: "How can you improve your results?"
- Three numbered columns (01, 02, 03 with colored top-bar separators):
  - **Keep**: What elements of your approach proved most effective?
  - **Change**: What specific modifications would you make next time?
  - **Add**: What new ideas from discussion would you integrate?
- Timer icon + time (typically 2 minutes)
- Used after the Show/Best Practices segment and before "Now Try Again" in two-attempt activities.
- **Sets up the second attempt.** Participants process what they learned before retrying.

### 8c. Let's Reflect Slide, What/So What/Now What (Single-Attempt, after Try)
- Title: "Let's reflect"
- Three cards with colored left borders (maroon, blue, green):
  - **What?** What happened? What did you get?
  - **So what?** What worked? What felt off?
  - **Now what?** What would you change next time?
- Used as the final reflection in single-attempt activities.
- This structure repeats identically for every single-attempt activity.

### 9. Sample Prompt Slide
- Title: "Sample prompt" or "Sample prompt - [step name]" when multiple prompts are needed
- Gray box containing the full prompt text
- Sometimes with color-coded annotations showing prompt components (Task, Input, Purpose, Format)
- Sometimes with output shown alongside (treemap, comparison table)
- **Multiple prompts:** When the approach has distinct steps that each need their own prompt (e.g., "Extracting facts" and "Building a project brief"), show them as separate "Sample prompt" slides with step-specific titles. Each prompt gets its own slide.

### 10. Best Practices Slide
- Title: "Best practices"
- Two columns:
  - **Pro tips** (maroon header): 3-5 short tactical bullets
  - **Limitations** (maroon header): 3-5 honest limitation bullets
- Gray callout box at bottom with key warning or tip (bold colored lead-in)
- **Every single-attempt activity ends with one of these.** In two-attempt activities, best practices are folded into the "Show" slide (type 18) instead.

### 11. Quote / Research Slide
- Large teal quotation marks framing the quote block
- Teal-bordered box containing the quote text with **bold key phrases**
- Citation at bottom: Author, "Title", Source, Date
- Used for external validation (Gallup data, research)

### 12. Comparison / Matrix Slide
- Cards in grid (2x2 or 3-across) with colored left borders
- Each card: bold colored header + description bullets
- Example: "Three types of agents" with No-code / Low-code / High-code columns
- Example: "Key considerations" in 2x2 grid: Security / Governance / Data / Investment

### 13. What's Next Slide
- Title: "What's next"
- Numbered columns (1, 2, 3) with colored number badges
- Each column: bold colored name + subtitle + timing
- Optional callout box with preview details

### 14. Thank You Slide
- Small teal label: "For your time and contributions"
- Large gray: "Thank you"
- Bulleted list of follow-up items (what they'll receive)
- Hero image right half

### 15. Questions Slide
- Full-width, minimal
- Small text with personality (workshop-specific, not fixed): "If we have time. We probably won't." / "You just tried something new." / or similar
- Giant gray: "Questions?"
- Optional sub-text: "Unmute or drop it in chat." (for virtual workshops)

### 16. How This Workshop Was Designed Slide
- Title: "How this workshop was designed"
- Timeline layout with two converging pathways:
  - **Grounded in Real Work**: activities built from actual tasks in participants' roles
  - **Built for You**: content tailored to this team's tools, workflows, and challenges
- Always slide 2 (immediately after title slide)
- Establishes credibility before stating purpose or outcomes

### 17. Check-In Slide
- Title: "Quick check"
- Single instruction line + callout box with one focused chat prompt
- Lightweight pulse after a second attempt, not a full reflection
- Example: "Drop one thing in the chat: What changed between your first attempt and your second?"
- In Two-Attempt arcs, the check-in is baked into Try 2 (thumbs up / down / side) rather than a separate slide

### 18. Show Slide (Sample Output + Best Practices)
- Title: varies by activity (e.g., "What Good Looks Like" or activity-specific)
- Shows a concrete sample output from the activity's task, then 2-3 best practices or watchpoints
- Positioned between Try 1 debrief and Try 2 in the Two-Attempt arc
- Purpose: give participants a reference point before their second attempt
- Replaces the old "Watchpoints" pattern. A concrete example is more useful than abstract pattern cards.
- Combine sample output and best practices into one slide when possible; split into two if density requires it

### 19. Crowd-source slide (in-person)

Use when the demo IS the room participating. The facilitator types into a live tool while the audience contributes verbally. The slide holds the seed and the audience prompt.

- **Banner:** "Build it together" (or similar collaborative label)
- **Title:** what's being built ("Brain dump: Spring brunch")
- **Starter list** (3-6 bullets the facilitator is seeding)
- **Audience prompt** as the call to action: "What else would you tell Claude?"
- Optional cap or count: "Cap at 6"
- No predicted output. The output happens in the live tool.

**Example:**
- Banner: "Build it together"
- Title: "Brain dump: Spring brunch"
- Bullets: Vibe, Audience, Vendors, Budget, Anti-patterns, What worked
- Audience prompt: "What else would you tell Claude?"

### 20. Live demo support slide

Slide that holds while the facilitator does live work in the tool (paste, edit, run a prompt, drag files). Names what's happening without revealing the result.

- **Title** naming the live action ("Swap in the full instructions", "Run the contract extraction")
- **One-line description** of what to watch for
- Optional **sample prompt** in a callout (the one being run)
- **No predicted output, no spoilers.** If the slide shows the result, the live demo loses its punch.

**Example for live instruction paste:**
- Title: "Swap in the full instructions"
- Description: "One line becomes a detailed block. Watch the Instructions field."

### 21. Leveled model slide

For frameworks that stack as levels (L1 to L2 to L3 to L4). Shows the whole stack on one slide. Use when the model is recurring throughout the workshop.

- **Title** naming the model ("Four levels of context")
- **Stacked or progressive visual** (cards, layers, ladder, ascending boxes)
- Each level labeled with **name + one-line description**
- Optional callout highlighting today's destination level
- Color or weight to indicate progression

**Example for Four levels of context:**
- Title: "Four levels of context"
- Four labeled cards: L1 In conversation / L2 Attachments / L3 Projects / L4 Directories
- Callout on L4: "Today we build here"

### 22. Triad / cycle slide

For three-part frameworks (three throughlines, three-step cycle, three pillars). Shows all three on one slide.

- **Title** naming the framework ("Three things matter" or "The feedback cycle")
- **Three labeled cards or arcs** (linear for a triad, circular for a cycle)
- Each card: **short label + one-line description**
- Optional **connector arrows** for cycles

**Examples:**
- Three throughlines: Context / Frameworks / Feedback loop
- Feedback cycle: Frame / Feedback / Capture (as a loop with arrows)

### 23. Compounding / foundation slide (closing)

For closes that emphasize daily building rather than one-time setup. Visual metaphor of foundation strengthening or blocks accumulating.

- **Title:** "Build the foundation" or "One block at a time"
- **Visual:** blocks stacking, foundation deepening, ascending timeline
- **Body:** "One block a day. Foundation gets stronger." Or similar single-line landing
- **No CTA on this slide.** The next slide handles the ask.

**Example for the close:**
- Title: "Build the foundation"
- Visual: stacking blocks growing taller over a timeline
- Body: "One block a day. The AI you're working with three months from now is dramatically more useful."

## Arc Slide Sequences

The canonical arc definitions, timings, and the single-vs-two-attempt decision live in `methodology/activity-arcs.md` (project root). This section is the slide-specific rendering: which slide types, in what order.

### Activity Arc: Single-Attempt (15-20 min)

The default arc. Use when participants need one pass through a tool or task.

1. **Section divider**: Activity N + Name + Tagline + Hero Image
2. **Problem slide**: The Problem: [Noun] + contrast pair
3. **Approach slide**: How To [X] + numbered steps
4. **Tool orientation**: (only if participants need to navigate a new tool surface)
5. **Sample prompt**: Full prompt in gray box
6. **Your turn**: Instructions + timer
7. **Reflect**: What? / So what? / Now what?
8. **Best practices**: Pro tips + Limitations

### Activity Arc: Two-Attempt (20-30 min)

Use when iteration is the learning. The first attempt surfaces what participants don't yet know; the compare, show, and second attempt let them apply what they've learned. The activity ends on doing, not discussing.

1. **Section divider**: Activity N + Name + Tagline + Hero Image
2. **Problem slide**: The Problem: [Noun] + contrast pair (2 min)
3. **Approach slide**: How To [X] + numbered steps (4 min)
4. **Tool orientation**: (only if participants need to navigate a new tool surface)
5. **Your turn**: Instructions + timer. Sample prompt shared in chat, not as a standalone slide. (4 min)
6. **Let's share**: What? / So what? / Now what? Facilitator-led debrief after first attempt. (6 min)
7. **Best practices**: Sample output + themes/watchpoints. Concrete reference point. (3 min)
8. **Sample prompt(s)**: Full prompt(s) in gray box. Shown after best practices as a reference before retry. Multiple slides if the approach has distinct steps.
9. **Let's reflect**: Keep / Change / Add. Participants process what they learned before retrying. (2 min)
10. **Now try again**: Updated instructions + shorter timer. Start a new conversation, write from scratch. (3 min)

Notes:
- Steps 7-8 can be reordered depending on the activity. Some show best practices first, then sample prompts; others combine them. The key requirement is that participants see a concrete reference point before their second attempt.
- The Let's Share (step 6) and Let's Reflect (step 9) are distinct slides with different structures. Let's Share surfaces what happened; Let's Reflect prepares for the retry.

### Opening Arc

**Standard (declarative):**
1. Title slide
2. Workshop design credibility ("How this workshop was designed")
3. Purpose / outcomes (2-3 pillars)
4. External validation (research quote)
5. Framing slide (dual lens, key distinction, or mental model)
6. Structure overview ("How we'll work")

**Demo-led (experiential):**
1. Title slide
2. Workshop design credibility ("How this workshop was designed")
3. Purpose / outcomes (2-3 pillars, with key framing woven into facilitator delivery, e.g., "not for craft" is spoken, not on the slide)
4. Demo section divider + demo: live, fast, visual proof of concept. Followed by a pattern slide that names the throughline (e.g., "You bring orientation. You name the task. You review the output."). Demo and pattern are combined into one segment; the pattern slide is what stays on screen as the anchor.
5. Structure overview ("How we'll work": activities, consistent pattern, participation norms)

Use the demo-led arc when the audience is skeptical or nervous. The demo does the framing work experientially: they see what AI does before you tell them the structure. The research quote and standalone framing slide from the standard arc get cut; the demo replaces both.

### Closing Arc

1. Section divider (Closing Activity)
2. Reflection activity (structured prompts for attendees)
3. Key takeaways (1-2 message slides)
4. What's Next (upcoming sessions, timeline)
5. Thank You (follow-up items)
6. Questions

## In-Person Workshop Adaptations

The slide taxonomy was developed for VILT (virtual instructor-led training). For in-person workshops, adjust:

- **Sample prompts in larger text.** Readable from the back of the room (~24pt minimum). Code-block style with high contrast.
- **Title slide includes the QR code** for shared materials access. Prominent on the right side or as a corner card. Visible while the room arrives.
- **Pair share slides specify "turn to a neighbor"** not "go into breakouts."
- **Crowd-source slides (#19)** are more useful in-person. Verbal contributions are easier than chat.
- **Live demo support slides (#20)** are critical. The room is watching one shared screen, not their own tool. The slide tells them what to watch for.
- **Timer and step count more prominent on Your Turn slides** (#7). 35+ people need clear pacing cues.
- **Drop the "drop in chat" cues.** Replace with "call it out" or "raise a hand."
- **QR code remains visible** (small corner placement) throughout the workshop in case someone arrives late or needs to re-scan.
