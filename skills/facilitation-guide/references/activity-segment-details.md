# Activity Segment Details (Facilitation Rendering)

How the facilitation guide renders each segment of the two activity arcs. Arc definitions, timing tables, and the single-vs-two-attempt decision live in the canonical file: `methodology/activity-arcs.md` (project root). Load this file when writing the segment-by-segment talk tracks and facilitator directions for an activity.

## Two-Attempt Activity: Segment Details

**Problem framing:**
- Name the pain in their world, not in AI terms
- Make it visceral: something they've experienced this month
- Frame what AI does well here (1-2 sentences)
- Frame what AI can't do (1-2 sentences)

**Approach:**
- Teach the core concept (e.g., "two steps, not one")
- Show where the tool lives (quick orientation if needed)
- Show the prompt structure: key components to include
- Connect back to the pattern throughline

**Try 1:**
- **Setup (~1 min):** Share materials (email thread, transcript, brief) and prompt template. Set the timer. Name the fallback ("If you get stuck, ask for help" or "drop a question in the chat").
- **While they work:** Monitor chat. Don't check in mid-work unless the timer is long. Note who's stuck for targeted support.

**Compare and discuss:**
- **Quick pulse (~30 sec):** Thumbs up/sideways/down. Scan the room.
- **Peer sharing (~2-3 min):** Call on 1-2 people from each group. "Walk us through how you wrote your prompt. What do you think led to the result you got? What would you change?"
- Listen for themes. Name them when they surface.

**Show:**
- **Name the themes (~2-3 min):** As participants share, name the patterns (e.g., "AI fills silence", "Check the seams", "Scope"). If a theme doesn't come up organically, introduce it after the sharing.
- Show a sample of what good output looks like as a reference point.

**Try 2:**
- **Reflect first (~1-2 min):** "Take a minute to think about what you heard from your peers."
- **Retry (~3-4 min):** "Start a new conversation. Don't build on your first one. Write your prompt from scratch."
- **Quick check-in** at the end (optional): thumbs up/down/sideways, or a chat prompt.

## Single-Attempt Activity: Key Difference

Reflection and best practices come after the single try, not between attempts. There is no retry. The activity ends on reflection, not on doing. The Problem framing and Approach segments follow the same guidance as above; the Reflect segment uses the structured What / So What / Now What prompts, and Best Practices closes with pro tips plus honest limitations.
