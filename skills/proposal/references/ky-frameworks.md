## The 6-Element Pitch Framework (apply in Mode 4)

Every pitch deck the skill produces must hit all six elements:

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Narrative** | Painful problem(s) to compelling transformation for your Lighthouse Clients |
| **Alignment** | Conceptual agreement on desired outcome using your Blue Ocean Services |
| **Proof** | Definitive results and social proofing for similar Lighthouse Clients |
| **Process** | How to repeatably drive outcomes for this prospect as a Lighthouse Client |
| **Offer** | Your Offer Portfolio to get an easy "I'm in" |
| **Timing** | Clarity of schedule and next steps |

**Mellonhead addition (not one of Ken's six):** the proposal skill always inserts a **Why Now** urgency-alignment beat right after Narrative and before Proof, distinct from the closing Timing. Ken folds urgency into Timing; Mellonhead surfaces it early as a fast "are we aligned this is urgent now?" check. See the proposal SKILL.md, Mode 4.

## Key principles to apply throughout

- **A pitch is NOT services. A pitch is NOT the proposal.** It's a structured way to walk the prospect through "Why You."
- **Don't lob proposals over the fence.** Send the proposal *right after* the pitch meeting, never before. Mode 6 enforces this.
- **Conceptual agreement before pitching the rest.** The Alignment slide is the gate. Talking points must explicitly pause for confirmation.
- **Lighthouse Client targeting.** Define the user's Lighthouse profile early; flag if a prospect doesn't fit. Lighthouse + first engagement = candidate for introductory pricing.
- **Blue Ocean Services.** Identify the user's differentiated offerings (the ones competitors can't easily copy). Lead the pitch's Process section with these.
- **Phase 1 must prove value in 3 months.** Even for longer engagements, build a 3-month value-proof point.
- **Composition derived from brief, not template.** Each prospect's Phase 1 looks different. Don't default.
- **Sunset the word "fit."** Help prospects become a good fit. Don't demean.
- **Cameras on.** All meetings are video calls (KY lesson 23).

## Patterns we've validated through real engagements

- **Proof slide pattern:** "[Number]+ professionals trained. Including [most-relevant audience] at [most-relevant client]. + [logos]." Keep tight.
- **Bio slide pattern:** "Bio in progress" placeholders should look intentional. Real bios lead with why this person is right for their specific role.
- **Methodology slide pattern:** Show production rigor (build-and-run cycle), not session arc preview.
- **Playbook / supplemental artifact pattern:** Group items into scale tiers (e.g., for-each-leader / for-their-teams / for-org-long-term). Two or three groups beats six bullets.
- **Investment pattern:** Partnership notes for introductory pricing as a footnote, not a banner. (A prior "two options, recommend one" pattern was removed here: it was encoded prematurely and is not KY guidance. KY's Offer element is simply an Offer Portfolio for an easy "I'm in"; option count and whether to emphasize one are per-deal calls.)
- **Closing question pattern:** "What's your reaction? What did I get right, what would you change?" Surfaces objections in the room.
