# ICP screen

> **v0.2, 2026-08-25.** Size bands, the professional-services call, and the override rule set by MQ. Values alignment added by MQ as a fourth test, scored. v0.1 was reverse-engineered from prior calls; this version is hers.

The source sentence, from the talk track in `strategy/what-we-sell.md`:

> "I work with mid-sized companies that don't have a big tech team driving AI. They've bought the tools; the part that isn't happening is people actually using them in their work."

Four tests. Size and sector are filters, buy signal sets the goal, values alignment predicts whether the work will land.

---

## Test 1: Size

**Sweet spot: 250 to 1,000.**

| Band | Call |
|---|---|
| Under 250 | **Weak.** Not enough organizational complexity for the problem to exist in the form Mellonhead solves. Budget is the real constraint: under 200 they probably cannot fund it, and anything that happened would have to be a small engagement. Not a hard kill, but say the budget risk out loud |
| 250 to 1,000 | **Core.** Complex enough to have the problem, central enough that HR owns the answer |
| 1,000 to 5,000 | **Depends on the mandate, not the headcount.** See below |
| Over 5,000 | **Probably too high** |

**The 1,000 inflection, and why it is really a mandate test.** Above roughly a thousand people, organizations tend to deploy an outside partner inside a *specific business unit* rather than through HR centrally. That changes the buyer, the scope, and the shape of the work.

So for anything between 1,000 and 5,000, read the req and ask: **is this mandate central or business-unit scoped?**

- **Central** reads as "enterprise AI transformation," "across the company," "all business units," sits on the People or HR team, and reports to a CHRO or CPO. {{prospect company}} at 1,480 qualifies on exactly this basis
- **Business-unit scoped** names one function, one division, or one product line, and reports into that unit

Central mandate keeps a larger company in play. Business-unit scope at that size means a different engagement than the one on the sales surface, and should be flagged rather than pursued by default.

**Override:** a strong buy signal can override a size miss in either direction. Say which rule is being overridden and why.

## Test 2: Sector

**Hard disqualifiers:**

1. **Sells AI consulting, AI education, or AI enablement services.** A competitor, not a buyer, and their internal program is usually their sales proof point. CDW is the clean case: 15,000 people, sells Copilot deployment and end-user training, and its own AI Champion network is the case study it sells with.
2. **A big internal tech team already driving AI.** This is the test from the talk track and it is about **capacity, not function.** The question is whether someone inside already has the people to do this work, not which title owns it.

   **Technology ownership is not, by itself, a disqualifier** (MQ ruling, 2026-08-31, superseding the earlier wording). At the sizes in the sweet spot a CIO or CISO typically has a small IT team and no enablement capability at all, and a Head of AI at that size is often **fractional** and in the middle of assembling a team. Those are buyers, and reasonable ones. The earlier version of this rule killed them on the org chart, which was wrong.

   **What actually fires this test** is an internal function with the capacity to deliver enablement itself: a staffed AI enablement or adoption team, a large L&D organization with an AI remit, or a technology org running its own training arm. A FinTech with a Technology org can still pass if nobody owns capability. A 20,000-person manufacturer whose CIO already has change-management and training functions does not.

   **The test to apply:** does this owner have a *capability* arm, or only a *delivery* arm? Engineers and a policy is a delivery arm. Ask it of whoever owns AI, whatever their title.
3. **Startups.** Explicitly out.
4. **Fractional practices and solo consultancies.** Not employers.
5. **Law firms. Added by MQ 2026-09-01.** Kill at pre-screen, before the body is recovered. **The offer does not fit as built:** enablement and curriculum are designed around how work gets done in a corporate function, and legal practice does not run that way. MQ's read comes from a close friend with a decade of law operations experience, not from the archive.

   **But record every one of them.** A law firm hiring for AI is a real signal of AI investment and the reqs are unusually explicit about the work, so the sector is a rich language and market-intelligence source even though it will never be a buyer. Verdict is **Evidence**, not a bare rejection: capture the text, write the index row and the signal-log line, and skip buyer research entirely. This is the one disqualifier where the capture is worth more than the screen.

**Not a disqualifier, but worth noting:** professional services and advisory firms (accounting, consulting). **Law firms moved to disqualifier 5 on 2026-09-01** and are no longer covered by this yellow flag. For the rest, MQ has no strong view either way. The observed pattern is that they rank low, which is what put Plante Moran, CohnReznick, and Arnold & Porter at the bottom of the 8/25 list, and the likely reason is that they staff analogous work internally and sell billable expertise. Treat as a yellow flag to name, not a reason to stop.

**Observed positive pattern:** regulated, mature, mid-sized firms in banking, credit unions, insurance carriers, asset and investment management, consumer finance, distribution, and manufacturing.

## Test 3: Buy signal

Evidence they will pay for outside help rather than solve it entirely with a hire.

**Strong:**
- The req is scoped as an engagement rather than a role. "Scope of the Engagement," "as a consultant," "a consultant's instinct"
- A self-disclosed capacity constraint. "The People team at PROG is lean" answers the build objection before it is raised
- One hire carrying an enterprise-wide mandate. That workload creates outside demand rather than replacing it
- A history of using outside vendors for training or change work

**Weak or absent:**
- A conventional FTE req with no scope language and no stated constraint
- A req sitting inside a large existing L&D function with capacity to absorb it

A weak buy signal does not kill the lead. It downgrades the goal from "complement the hire" to "relationship only," and it can no longer be used to override a size miss.

## Test 4: Values alignment (scored)

**Why this is in the ICP and not a preference.** Each dimension is a precondition for the delivery model to work, not only a statement about who MQ wants to work with.

Score each dimension 0 to 3. Total 0 to 9.

### Where the evidence comes from, in weight order

1. **The req's own language.** Highest weight. This is the company talking about the actual work, unprompted, and it is where framing shows
2. **The buyer's published writing.** High weight. What they argue for in public they will defend internally
3. **Stated company values, EVP, careers-page copy.** Low weight on its own. Everyone claims innovation and collaboration. It counts when it is specific, and it counts more when the req independently echoes it

**Counter-signals are more diagnostic than positive ones.** Any company will say the right words somewhere. What separates them is whether the req frames people as the point or as a cost.

### The three dimensions

**1. People as strategy (0 to 3)**

They want business outcomes *through the people they have today.* Their workforce is part of the strategy rather than a line item the strategy acts on.

- **Positive:** the req talks about people succeeding, being equipped, being supported through change. Development language. Retention and growth framed as the goal rather than the byproduct
- **Counter:** AI framed as headcount reduction, cost takeout, FTE savings, or "doing more with less." Efficiency as the only stated outcome
- **Why it matters for delivery:** determines whether the program survives the first budget tightening, and whether managers are given time for it

**2. Innovation (0 to 3)**

Experimentation is permitted and rewarded, not just tolerated.

- **Positive:** language about trying, learning, iterating, redesigning work rather than optimizing it. Tolerance for ambiguity named as a requirement
- **Counter:** compliance-and-risk-only framing. Perpetual "pilot" language with no commitment. AI framed defensively, as something to control rather than to use
- **Why it matters for delivery:** champions have nothing to model if the culture punishes trying things. The program becomes a training calendar

**3. Collaboration (0 to 3)**

Work crosses functions by default.

- **Positive:** cross-functional partners named explicitly. Shared ownership between HR, Talent, and Technology. Mentorship, peer learning, or community structures already in place
- **Counter:** siloed ownership, IT alone owning AI, no partner functions named, a req that reads as one team's project
- **Why it matters for delivery:** the champions model is explicitly cross-team. Champions are shared across groups. Without a collaborative culture the structural premise of the program does not hold

### Bands

| Total | Read |
|---|---|
| **7 to 9** | Strong alignment. Promote, and use their own language in the outreach |
| **4 to 6** | Workable. Name the weak dimension in the brief; it is usually where the engagement will strain |
| **2 to 3** | Weak. Downgrade the goal to relationship only. Do not build an engagement thesis on it |
| **0 to 1** | Walk, even if size, sector, and buy signal all pass. The work will not land |

**A worked example of a high score.** {{prospect company}}'s posting carries an explicit Employee Value Proposition naming "opportunity for inclusive collaboration, opportunity for innovation, and opportunity for development," and the req independently echoes all three: people "set up to succeed," managers equipped for "the tougher conversations," and Talent, HR, and Technology named as partners. The buyer's own published work argues for mutual mentorship and cross-functional development. Three dimensions corroborated across all three evidence sources, which is what a 3 looks like rather than a 1.

**This list is open.** MQ named these three as the ones she could articulate now. Add a dimension when a real engagement shows a fourth thing mattered.

## Combining the tests

Not a simple all-must-pass. In order:

1. **Sector disqualifier** kills the lead outright. No override.
2. **Values 0 to 1** kills it too, whatever else passes.
3. **Size miss** can be overridden by a strong buy signal. Name the override.
4. **Weak buy signal** downgrades the goal rather than killing the lead, and removes the size override.
5. **Values 2 to 3** downgrades the goal to relationship only.

## Fit grade (added 2026-09-01, MQ)

The verdict answers "is this a prospect." **The grade answers "does this deserve MQ's Tuesday."** Two accounts can both be Prospect and be nothing alike: on 2026-08-31 Banc of California and Match Group both came back "Prospect, borderline" with no way to rank one above the other, which is what this fixes.

Every screened company gets one, including the ones that fail. Grade from the four tests already run; do not introduce a new judgment.

| Grade | When | What MQ does with it |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | Size in the sweet spot, sector clean, **strong** buy signal, values 7 or higher | Worth her calendar. Send before the window moves |
| **Medium** | Passes every test, with exactly one soft: a weak buy signal, a size override, or values 4 to 6 | Real, with a named caveat. Send when the High list is clear |
| **Low** | Two or more soft, or a Park, or a named structural caveat (an incumbent vendor, a population that is the inverse of the reader) | Keep warm. Not worth a planning slot |
| **None** | Any disqualifier fired: sector, or values 0 to 1 | Screened out. Recorded so the company is not researched again in a month |

**A Park is never higher than Low.** Right fit, wrong timing means it is not this week's work whatever its other scores say.

**Name the soft dimension in the grade line.** "Medium: weak buy signal" is useful; "Medium" alone throws away the reason and forces a re-read of the whole brief.

**Write the grade into `business-development/outreach/leads.tsv`** along with the tests behind it. That file is the queryable record; the brief is the prose. `operations/build-leads.py` regenerates the readable view grouped by grade.

## Recording the verdict

One of four, written to `business-development/outreach/signal-log.md`:

- **Prospect** — promote after the recency check
- **Park** — passes on fit, wrong timing. Needs a date, never an open-ended hold
- **Not a prospect** — name which test failed, so the company is not re-researched in a month
- **Evidence** — fails as a buyer, but the signal is worth keeping. CDW is the model
